ENTRY 001

The schema was premature.

A generic GameState was about to encode untested assumptions. The project stepped back to define the prediction question and inspect real source contracts first.

ENTRY 002

The first target needed an auditable answer.

A/B/No Plant replaced a vague first goal such as recognizing an execute. The objective event made labels, leakage checks, baselines, and calibration assessable.

ENTRY 003

A plausible parser field was not enough.

The first site-label audit found disagreement with plant events. Source outputs became claims to check, not automatic ground truth.

ENTRY 004

A bomb event could be true and still be stale.

A drop event describes where the bomb was dropped. After pickup, current inventory is more informative about where it is now. The reconstruction policy changed.

ENTRY 005

Zero spread changed the interpretation, not the arithmetic.

A lone survivor also has a compact geometric footprint. Combat state was needed to distinguish survival from coordinated formation.

ENTRY 006

Useful-sounding information could lose its place.

Current equipment value was tested in both model families. It worsened probability quality, so economy was excluded from the selected baseline.

ENTRY 007

The model and the representation had to be questioned together.

Defense did not improve logistic probability metrics. The nonlinear comparison recovered predictive value, motivating the interaction-dependent interpretation.

ENTRY 008

The errors exposed a different next question.

Most errors crossed Plant/No Plant. Task-specific importance linked combat to feasibility and geometry to site choice, motivating a hierarchical hypothesis.

ENTRY 009

Completion meant freezing a boundary.

The offline investigation became a recorded V0 baseline. Replay and synthetic inference are validated; real-CS2 capture remains deferred. V1 subsequently tested hierarchy and temporal features without replacing V0.

V2 debugging journey

The important change was not only fixing bugs. It was changing how the project thinks about data.

Entry 005

Untouched data challenged assumptions development data never challenged.

The confirmation corpus did not immediately tell us whether the model generalized. First, it showed that our adapter assumptions were incomplete. That is exactly why untouched data mattered.

Entry 006

Evidence had to be allowed to overturn our own debugging story.

We first suspected bomb-event round-number misalignment. The audit returned zero mismatches. Then we treated round-level bomb-site metadata as expected truth. 298 independent plant/place checks showed that assumption was wrong too.

Entry 007

The legacy bug was real, but measurable rather than catastrophic.

Ghost plants affected 44 / 1686 labels, not the entire feature system. Measuring exact scope let us repair supervision without redesigning the model.

Entry 008

Debugging became part of the architecture.

Pickup gaps, snapshot gaps, ghost plants, unresolved labels, and unavailable bomb states are now explicit integrity contracts. The goal is for the next strange demo to be classified automatically before it becomes another manual investigation.

What V2 taught us

The most important V2 result was not a larger model. It was learning to protect the experiment.

A confirmation dataset first exposed problems in the data pipeline, not superiority in a model. We repaired the pipeline before looking at predictions, rebuilt the development evidence without retuning, froze the corrected models, and only then opened D_CONFIRM once.

The result was deliberately uneventful: V0 remained slightly better on the primary metric. That is useful evidence. The project did not promote H2 simply because H2 was more complicated or because one component of it calibrated better during development.
